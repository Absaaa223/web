# DUST — Narrative

## The rules, before the story

These are absolute. Every one has been violated in a draft and every violation
made the game worse.

1. **No cutscenes.** Not at the start, not at the end, not at a discovery.
2. **No narrator, no radio voice, no companion.** The player is alone.
3. **Nothing is ever explained by the game.** It is only ever *shown*.
4. **No collectible-log dumps.** An inscription is one sentence, sometimes one
   word, occasionally one image. Never a page.
5. **Order is not guaranteed.** Any piece must be findable first and still mean
   something. Nothing may depend on having read something else.
6. **The ending is open and the game does not tell you it has ended.**

Rule 5 is the hardest and the most important. It means the story cannot be a
sequence. It has to be a *set of pieces that resolve into different but
compatible readings depending on the order you find them*.

## The Vessel Age

What follows is the writers' reference. **None of it is ever stated in the
game.** The player assembles some fraction of it, and different players will
assemble different fractions.

### What happened

A civilisation lived here when it was not desert. They were not more advanced
than us in most ways — no flying cars, no energy weapons — but they were
extraordinary at one thing: **moving water**.

Their whole culture was hydrological engineering. Aqueducts, cisterns, sub-surface
qanats, condensation towers. Ashkarrid, the buried city, was built around a
distribution hub the size of a cathedral. Their art is full of water. Their
writing measures time in flow rates.

They did not run out of water. They ran out of the *ability to agree about it*.

The evidence in the world: three generations of infrastructure, each one
technically superb and each one incompatible with the last. Junctions where a
newer aqueduct simply terminates against an older one at a slightly wrong angle.
Cisterns sealed from the inside. A vast condensation array in the Spine that was
built, completed, and never connected to anything.

The desert is not a catastrophe. It is what a place looks like when the people
in it spent four hundred years each building their own correct solution.

### The vessels

The recurring object, and the game's title.

Ceramic water vessels, found everywhere, in every region, in every context.
Ubiquitous, ordinary, and the single most reliable narrative thread because they
are the one thing that appears across all three generations.

- **First generation:** large, communal, unmarked. One vessel served many people.
- **Second generation:** smaller, individually marked with an owner's sign.
- **Third generation:** smallest, sealed, carried on the body, marked with a sign
  *and* a capacity, and increasingly often, a second sign scratched out.

A player who notices nothing else will still, over forty hours, notice that the
vessels get smaller. That is the story, delivered entirely through the size of a
pot, and it works regardless of what order the pots are found in.

### The last people

They did not all die. Most of them walked.

The Veil — the permanent storm belt — is on the route out. What is found there is
the debris of an enormous, slow, badly-provisioned departure: not bodies, but
things people put down because they could no longer carry them. Vessels among
them, mostly empty, mostly the small sealed kind.

### The one who stayed

There is a second thread, much sparser, that a thorough player may assemble.

Someone stayed. They lived a long time after. Their traces are unmistakable
because they are the only marks in the world made *without* the civilisation's
conventions — no formal script, no measurement, no owner-sign. Charcoal, not
carving. Practical, not ceremonial.

They kept one of the first-generation vessels. The large communal kind. It is
found, intact, at the deepest point of the cave system, in a chamber they
clearly lived in for years.

Whether they were the last of that civilisation, or someone who arrived
afterwards, or the player character before the player character woke — the game
does not say, cannot be made to say, and three drafts that tried to say it were
each worse than saying nothing.

### The player

Who the player is, why they woke in the sand, whether they have been here before:
**never established.** There is no reveal, no amnesia plot, no final document.

The player's identity is deliberately the one question the world contains no
evidence about. Every other mystery in DUST has enough material to construct an
answer. This one has none, on purpose, so that the player's interpretation
belongs to them and is never contradicted.

## How pieces are delivered

| Vector | Density | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture and infrastructure | Everywhere | Reads as fact, not story. Most players never "read" it consciously and absorb it anyway |
| Vessels | ~450 | The reliable thread. Order-independent by design |
| Inscriptions | ~900 | One or two sentences. Formal script, measured, bureaucratic |
| Charcoal marks | ~40 | The one who stayed. Informal, practical, human |
| Terrain itself | Ashkarrid | The street grid readable from the ground before any wall is found |
| Fauna behaviour | Ongoing | Species that evolved around the *infrastructure* — nesting in cisterns, drinking from aqueduct seeps |

That last one deserves emphasis: several species have adapted to the ruins.
A player who watches a Pale Fennec long enough sees it drinking from a seep in a
broken aqueduct that still, after all this time, carries a trickle. That is a
narrative beat delivered entirely by the ecology simulation, and it is
discoverable by patient observation rather than by finding a document.

## The ending

There is no ending event, no credits trigger, no final chamber.

The deepest cave chamber with the communal vessel is the closest thing the game
has to a destination, and reaching it changes nothing mechanically. The player
can walk back out and keep exploring. Most will.

What the game does have is a **last thing worth walking to** for any given
player, and the curiosity director's job is to make sure that thing is always
somewhere ahead of them for as long as they want to keep going.

Players stop playing DUST. They do not finish it. The distinction is the entire
design.

## Writing standards

- Inscriptions: 12 words maximum. Most are shorter.
- No proper nouns except Ashkarrid, and that one is only ever inferred from
  context, never labelled.
- No dates. The civilisation measured time in flow rates; where a date appears,
  it appears as a volume.
- Charcoal marks are never full sentences. They are lists, tallies, and once, a
  drawing.
- If a piece of text explains something, cut it. If it *implies* two
  incompatible things, keep it.
